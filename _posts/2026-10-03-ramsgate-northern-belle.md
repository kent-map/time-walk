The Northern Belle was an American Transatlantic ship built in 1854. Early in January 1857, under Captain Tite; Master of The Vessel, the ship laden with cargo, was parted from her anchor and driven onto rocks. The ‘Belle’ was seen from the shore at daylight, with 23 people lashed to the ship’s remaining mast.  
Three Luggers the ‘Ocean’,’ Victory’ and ‘Eclipse’ went initially to assist The Northern Belle. 5 seamen from these Luggers, were put onboard the Belle to aid the floundering ship. 
2 Lifeboat Luggers followed these Luggers; the ‘Mary White’ and the ‘Culmer White’ The 2 Lifeboats had to be manhandled and dragged by horses for 2 miles to a launching site at Kingsgate Bay. The route involved crossing the hilly terrain (covered in snow) with North Easterly gale force winds impeding their journey. The “Mary White” and the “Culmer White” made 3 attempts to rescue the ship and those clinging to life aboard the Belle.
During the valiant rescue operation, the “Victory” was swamped by the heavy seas and sank, taking with her, its crew of 9 sailors and fishermen. These men, all from Margate, left their widows and more than 40 children fatherless. The whole incident was reported Nationally and Internationally. The cost of their service, to help other sailors was immeasurable, for their loved ones and for their community. In recognition of all the seamen involved, the US Consul in London raised funds for the widows and children. Those involved with this heroic rescue received a silver medal, bible and money from the President of the USA. All aboard the ‘Belle’ were finally rescued by horse drawn Surf boats and taken ashore.
